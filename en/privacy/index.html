ESHArio Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 September 2026. This page describes the ESHArio browser extension for Google Chrome, the eshario.com website and the purchase of a key.

Short version

ESHArio keeps your data on your own computer. It leaves by two roads, and you switch on both yourself: settings into Chrome's own cloud when you press the button (see "Chrome's own cloud") and your email and password to our server when you sign in to an account (see "Accounts and paid access"). Without that press, without signing in and without asking us to reset your password, the extension sends nothing about you anywhere. It never sees your EA account password. There is no analytics, no tracking and no advertising. We do not sell your data and do not share it for anyone else's advertising. To run the service, some of the data is handled by our suppliers: the payment partner, hosting and a mail service (see the section "The website and your purchase"). When you buy, we receive your email and order number from the payment partner, only to deliver the access and answer your emails.

What ESHArio is

ESHArio is an extension for Google Chrome that adds tools inside the official EA SPORTS FC Web App at https://www.ea.com/*/ultimate-team/web-app*. It runs on that address and nowhere else. On every other page it does nothing at all: no script is injected and no request is made.

ESHArio is an independent product. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Electronic Arts Inc.

What is stored on your device

ESHArio uses Chrome's storage permission to keep its own data in your browser's local storage. There is one exception, described below under "Chrome's own cloud", and you switch it on yourself. The data consists of:

  • Your settings. Which features are on, hotkey assignments, display choices, and the state of panels you opened or closed.
  • Your saved filter sets. The market searches you chose to save so you can repeat them in one click.
  • A local copy of your club list. A copy of the cards in your club, read from the Web App, so squads can be assembled without asking EA for the same list over and over. It holds card identifiers, ratings and similar game attributes. It holds nothing about you as a person.
  • A purchase log. The price you paid for a card, kept locally because EA discards that number itself and it cannot be recovered afterwards.
  • Safety counters. How many market searches were made today, and how many purchases and Squad Building Challenges were made in the last hour and the last day. These counters enforce the built-in limits and must survive a page reload, otherwise pressing F5 would reset them and the limits would be meaningless.
  • Reference data read from the Web App. A catalogue of card definitions and the rarity groups EA uses, kept locally so the extension does not have to re-read them on every load.
  • A licence key, if you bought one and entered it: the string license.key in local storage. The key is signed and verified on your own machine, is never sent anywhere, and says nothing about you.

Chrome stores it on the machine where the extension is installed.

Chrome's own cloud

Chrome has a built-in store of its own that it carries between the computers where you are signed in to your Google account. The extension can use it so that you do not have to set your preferences again on a second computer.

It never does so on its own. Until you press "Sync settings" in the "Chrome's cloud" section of the settings window at least once, nothing goes into that store and the extension does not even read it. After your first press, later changes travel there by themselves.

Seven groups travel: feature toggles, saved filter sets, the limits and options for buying, the options for selling, the interface state and pack locks. The licence key, the local club mirror, the purchase log and the safety counters never travel there.

That store belongs to your Google account, not to us: we do not look into it and receive nothing from it. How Google handles it is set out in Google's own terms. To take our settings out of it, remove the extension on every computer or clear the extension's data in Chrome.

What is sent over the network

ESHArio makes network requests to exactly two third-party sites (four host patterns in the manifest), and only to read public reference data. The fourth pattern is not a third party at all: it belongs to our own account server, described under "Accounts and paid access" below. Every request to those third-party addresses is sent with cookies omitted, so nothing that identifies you or your browser session travels with it. What goes to our own server is described under "Accounts and paid access": there an identifier does travel, and it is your own session token.

AddressWhat ESHArio requestsWhat it says about you
r2.fut.ggFUT.GG's public manifest file and the public price files it names, for the platform you selectednothing
www.fut.gg/apithe public list of active Squad Building Challenges and community ratings for them, by page number and challenge idnothing

Both addresses belong to FUT.GG, a public site with FUT market prices. Prices live there as one shared file for everyone, and that is the file ESHArio downloads. What leaves your computer is only a page number and the id of the Squad Building Challenge you opened. No card from your club, no EA email and no account number goes there: FUT.GG does not know who asked.

That is the complete list of THIRD-PARTY sites. Besides them ESHArio contacts one server of our own, api.eshario.com, and only when you sign in to an account yourself, ask to reset a password or press "Sync settings"; while you are signed in it also asks that server from time to time whether your paid access is open: when the web app opens, when you come back to it and when you press "Refresh". What goes there is described below, under "Accounts and paid access".

ESHArio has no analytics and no telemetry. There is no usage reporting, no crash reporting to us and no advertising network. Requests to EA and to FUT.GG carry no identifier OF OURS: requests to EA's servers are the ones the web app itself makes with your own EA session, and requests to fut.gg go out with no cookies and with nothing of yours attached.

Requests to our own server carry the session token after you sign in; signing in itself and password recovery go without it. When you sign in, your email, your password (checked and forgotten: only an irreversible fingerprint stays on the server), a random device name the extension invents for itself, which is tied to nothing but our own extension, and a short device label such as "Windows · Google Chrome" go there too. Press "Sync settings" and those same seven groups of settings go there as well. No other information about you is in these requests.

What ESHArio never touches

  • Your EA credentials. ESHArio does not read, store, transmit or fill in your EA email address, your password, or your two-factor code. You sign in yourself, in EA's own form. The only thing ESHArio presses on its own is the platform choice after you are already signed in, which saves one click.
  • Your cookies and your EA session. ESHArio does not read cookies. It does not request the cookies permission and cannot.
  • Your browsing history. ESHArio does not request the history or tabs permission and has no way to see what other pages you visit.
  • Other websites. ESHArio has no permission to run on any site other than the EA Web App and the two read-only sites above, plus our own account server api.eshario.com, which it also writes to: sign-in, access rights, password recovery and the settings you sync by pressing the button.
  • Your clipboard's contents. ESHArio can place a diagnostic report into your clipboard when you ask it to. It never reads what is already there.

Accounts and paid access

ESHArio is planned as a free set of features plus paid access for a term, opened either in an account or by a licence key. In this version the account service is enabled. You sign in yourself, inside the extension itself and never on an EA page: you type your email address and password there, and they go to our server api.eshario.com. What comes back is a signed statement of your paid access and a session token; the token is kept on your device and never reaches the EA Web App page. A short device label travels with the sign-in: the platform and the browser, such as "Windows · Google Chrome". That label is what the "new device" email names and what the limit of two devices per account counts. Your password is not stored on our server as itself, only as an irreversible hash.

The server holds: your email address, an irreversible hash of your password (scrypt), device sessions, orders and an account event journal (what happened to the account and when: a sign-in, a sign-out, a renewal). A session lives 30 days without use and is extended by every use; two of them run at a time, one per device. The "set your password" link from the email lives 24 hours and is spent the moment it is used. The IP address is used only to rate-limit sign-in attempts and is not stored in the database. Order records are kept as long as Georgian tax accounting requires; an account lives until you ask us to delete it.

On your computer the extension keeps a signed snapshot of the access (it keeps the extension working offline for up to 7 days) and a session token. The session token goes to our account server only, in the request header; the EA page and any other website cannot reach it.

The extension's settings go to our server only if you press "Sync settings" in the "Cloud settings" section of the account page yourself. What travels is settings only: feature toggles, filter sets, the limits and options for buying and selling, the interface state, pack locks. Club lists, purchase journals, the account safety counters and the licence key never travel.

Until you sign in or ask to reset your password, not a single request goes to that server, and a licence key still opens the access: you enter it in the extension panel, its signature is checked on your own machine, and neither the key nor anything behind it leaves your computer.

Payment is handled by an external payment provider; ESHArio never sees or stores your card details.

The website and your purchase

The website eshario.com sets no cookies. It has two forms. The promo code field: the code you enter is kept in your browser's storage and is not sent anywhere. And the new-password form on the /password page, which the link in our email opens: the password you type goes once to our account server and nowhere else. The site is served through a hosting provider. Like any host, it keeps technical logs with visitors' IP addresses; they live with the host under its rules, and we do not read them. The visit counter, when enabled, works without cookies and without identifying the visitor.

Payment happens on the payment partner's page under its rules; we never see card details. From the partner we receive: the email given at checkout, the order number, the amount, the term, the promo code, the partner code, the date and the page language. Why: to deliver access, answer emails, make refunds, count the partner's reward and keep tax records. Emails about the key and the account are sent by a mail service from noreply@eshario.com; it sees the address and the text of the email. Order and account records are kept on our rented server; only we have access to it. Support emails arrive through email forwarding into our mailbox; Telegram conversations are stored by Telegram under its rules.

Sharing and selling

ESHArio does not sell your data, does not share it for anyone else's advertising, and does not use it to assess creditworthiness or for lending. There is nothing to sell: the extension sends our server only what your own account needs, and no one else anything. Settings that went into Chrome's own cloud when you pressed the button live in your own Google account, and we do not see them. Purchase data is used only for delivery, support, refunds and tax records, and is passed only to the suppliers named in the section "The website and your purchase", in the amount their work needs.

The use of information received through ESHArio adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

Data stays in your browser until you remove it. To delete all of it, remove the extension from chrome://extensions. Chrome deletes the extension's local storage with it. Removing the extension deletes nothing in your EA account. Retention for purchase and account data is set out in the section "The website and your purchase".

Children

ESHArio is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from them. Apart from the account email and the purchase data, the extension collects no personal data from anyone; access may be bought only by an adult.

Changes to this policy

If ESHArio's handling of data changes, this page is updated first, with a new date at the top, and the Chrome Web Store listing is updated at the same time.
